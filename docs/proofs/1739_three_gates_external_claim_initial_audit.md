## Follow-up: exact one-debit versus two-debit algebra

The cached source was checked through Lemma 6.28, Corollary 6.29, Lemma
6.61 and the full proof of Lemma 8.4. Lemma 6.61 defines

    Q=E*(1-c*u), D=E*(1-c)*u, 0<=u<=1,
    c=gamma*J, E=integral a*|x|^2.

Its conclusion Q-D=E*(1-u)>=0 is correct. Under the positive physical-pivot
hypothesis of Lemma 6.28, c<1. For the pure ground profile x proportional
to 1/a, u=1, E>0, and

    Q=D=E*(1-c)>0,
    Q-D=0, Q-2*D=-E*(1-c)<0.

Thus the one-debit identity cannot itself pay two identical debits. This is
an exact algebraic example within the scalar lemma, not a numerical probe
or a constructed counterexample on the paper's full arithmetic owner.

Lemma 8.4 explicitly says after (211) that the two-arm fold changes the
corresponding pivot by -2D. It then invokes Lemma 6.61 for MASTER-P3b, which
subtracts D once. The remaining audit question is precise: does an earlier
definition already allocate the other debit, or is there an independent
reserve sufficient to cover it on the SAME vector? The excerpts inspected
do not establish that allocation. The displayed direct target diagonal must
also be tracked because Theorem 8.5 uses it for parent losses; its existence
alone does not show an unused reserve.

Corollary 6.29 asserts the identification with the one-defect row; verifying
that assertion on the original operator remains necessary. No claim that
the pure-ground scalar example satisfies every global hypothesis is made.
Accordingly this is an unresolved proof-accounting gap in this audit, not a
definitive invalidity verdict on the paper. It provides no project sign
producer. The external mechanism remains attributed to the source and is
not an independent discovery of this project.
