## What is bounded

A carrier is the known oscillating factor exp(i theta y). Its envelope is the
remaining smooth amplitude exp(y/2) times the compact bump. Moving the carrier
into analytic moments avoids approximating that known oscillation with a
polynomial. The physical support radius is the square of the stored width.

Let M7 denote an upper bound for the magnitude of the envelope's seventh
derivative on a panel, and r its half-length. Degree-six Lobatto interpolation
has integrated remainder at most M7 r^8/(32*7!). The factor 1/32 is the exact
integral of the absolute nodal product, independently tested in record 2296.

Each panel is split into two derivative-enclosure cells. On each cell the
instrument takes the minimum of a direct seventh-derivative interval bound and
a centre seventh-derivative bound plus cell radius times an eighth-derivative
bound. Support-crossing cells use the flat-edge machinery of record 2293.
Coefficients of the same width within a carrier are combined as complex values;
carrier bounds are then triangle-summed. This deliberately loses cancellation
between different carriers, but still produces a small transform-only price.

## Independent controls

seventh/eighth derivatives against independent high-precision differentiation;
both support-edge signs and orders; the centred variation bound; complete

The derivative checks use an independently evaluated analytic bump, not a
second invocation of the interval jet formula. They test implementation
consistency; finitely many points are not a proof of universal containment.
