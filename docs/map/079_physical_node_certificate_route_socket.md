# 079 — Finite physical-kernel node certificate socket

Status: active formal consumer interface. The analytic signed certificate is
open.

Authority: subordinate to [003] and the current signed owner [080]. Consumer:
the detector-specific semi-local gate, then `SourceRH`.

## Owner and result

The socket keeps all quantities on one selected orbit detector:

- its actual compact support;
- the resulting finite visible-prime-power set;
- the actual Archimedean square term;
- physical kernel values and derivatives at the visible logarithmic nodes;
- a signed aggregate bound strong enough to imply
  `orbitWindowSemiLocalGate`.

The formal consumer prevents a future proof from mixing a node estimate from
one test, cutoff, carrier, or envelope with another.

## Formal assets

The physical-kernel stack provides:

- compactly supported point bumps and finite combinations;
- exact Mellin/Laplace readback for those combinations;
- finite interpolation interfaces for prescribed node values;
- square and cross-profile quadratic readbacks;
- finite gate-matrix and determinant consumers;
- an exact complex aggregate over the actual visible owner;
- factorization of that aggregate by the common reflected raw factor and the
  same-owner phase profile;
- post-aggregation norm and L2 bounds that preserve cancellation longer than
  primewise absolute-value estimates.

These are formal construction and readback statements. They do not assign the
needed sign to the aggregate.

## Required certificate

A successful certificate must prove, on the actual selected detector, either
the full semi-local inequality directly or fields implying:

```text
Archimedean remainder + signed finite physical-node aggregate <= 0.
```

The finite set must be the support-derived owner already carried by
`OrbitG8Geometry`. The estimate must include all visible powers and preserve
the detector's triple vanishing and off-line-zero detection.

## Rejected substitutions

- finite interpolation existence without a quantitative cost;
- pointwise signs imposed independently at all nodes without compatibility
  with the selected detector;
- a fixed-prime or continuous-density model;
- a sum of channelwise absolute values that loses the required cancellation;
- an assumption named “certificate” whose final field is the target sign.
